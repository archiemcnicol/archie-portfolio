# Website visual direction — working brief

## North star

The site should feel like a **personal creative studio**: visually confident enough
for a brand or agency, but clear and useful enough for a photography or business
client. It should not read as a CV, a generic influencer media kit or a collection
of unrelated landing pages.

The existing visual DNA is worth keeping:

- oversized, tightly set sans-serif headlines;
- warm paper, near-black and one sharp acid accent;
- thin rules and an editorial grid;
- real work and real metadata taking priority over decoration.

The final design should make that language feel more intentional and less like a
working prototype.

## Design principles

1. **Show the work early.** The first viewport should establish Archie’s point of
   view and give visitors a direct route into the relevant body of work.
2. **Editorial outside, commercial inside.** Public pages can have strong art
   direction; services, deliverables, dates and results should remain easy to find.
3. **One identity, different entry points.** Creator, photography, business and
   professional pages should feel related without becoming identical templates.
4. **Evidence before claims.** Logos, campaign links, analytics and case-study
   results should only appear when they are verified and appropriate to share.
5. **Restraint creates value.** Motion, colour and metadata should guide attention,
   not compete with the work.

## Proposed visual system

### Colour

Keep the current palette as the foundation:

- paper: `#F5F1EA`;
- ink: `#111111`;
- muted text: warm grey rather than neutral grey;
- acid accent: `#D8FF34`;
- occasional work-specific blue or stone surfaces.

Use the accent as a signal for action, selection or a memorable moment, not as a
background for every section. The page should remain recognisable in monochrome.

### Type and information hierarchy

- One distinctive, highly legible sans for display and body copy.
- Optional mono or condensed treatment for dates, platforms, post types and status.
- Headlines should be large, but supporting copy should breathe at a comfortable
  reading size.
- Public-facing copy should describe the work plainly; avoid internal terms such as
  “portfolio system” in the final navigation and footer.

### Layout and rhythm

- Keep the 12-column desktop grid and strong horizontal rules.
- Let each major page have one memorable composition rather than repeating the same
  hero, card grid and CTA in exactly the same order.
- Use generous vertical spacing around the work, with denser metadata only inside
  campaign and project modules.
- On mobile, preserve the editorial order: identity → work → proof → contact.

## Page direction

### Homepage

The homepage should introduce Archie before asking visitors to choose a route. The
next pass should add a carefully selected visual anchor—one real image or campaign
frame—alongside the current statement, then make the four audience paths feel like
clear doors rather than ordinary navigation cards.

Suggested order:

1. identity and visual anchor;
2. four ways in (brand work, photography, business, professional);
3. a small selection of work with distinct visual treatments;
4. concise proof and an uncomplicated contact action.

### Brand work

Keep the campaign list editorial and evidence-led. Real logos, TikTok covers and
analytics should sit together in a compact case-study unit. Views can remain marked
as unavailable until a trustworthy snapshot exists; likes should continue to show
their export date.

The public page should name the company that Archie worked with. Agencies and event
contexts belong in private evidence or supporting copy where they clarify the work,
not in the headline client label.

### Photography

Let the photographs lead. The archive count and metadata are useful proof, but they
should sit around the gallery rather than above it. The first edit can remain small
while the archive is reviewed; later groups should become project-led collections,
not an unfiltered dump of every file.

### Business and professional

These pages currently provide the right structure but still read as placeholders.
The next content pass should replace generic blocks with a few real, specific proof
points and use the same visual grammar as the photography and brand pages.

## Motion and interaction

- Keep hover movement slow and slight, mainly for image crops and links.
- Use reveal transitions only for major sections, and respect reduced-motion settings.
- Make cover images and logos feel integrated into the composition, not like embeds
  added after the copy.
- Avoid autoplay video, noisy gradients and interface effects that distract from
  the work.

## Build sequence

1. **Foundation:** finalise type scale, wordmark, navigation language, spacing and
   shared colour tokens.
2. **Homepage:** introduce the visual anchor and sharpen the four audience routes.
3. **Brand presentation:** finish logo/cover treatments, add verified analytics and
   build a deeper campaign detail pattern only when evidence is ready.
4. **Photography:** review the Drive archive, name coherent groups and promote the
   strongest images into project collections.
5. **Business and professional proof:** replace placeholder copy with selected work,
   results and working details.
6. **Private layers:** add role-aware analytics, rates, client areas and admin tools
   without leaking private evidence into public pages.

## Definition of “finished”

The site is visually ready when a first-time visitor can understand what Archie does
within one screen, reach the relevant work in one click, see real evidence without
being overwhelmed by numbers, and move from interest to contact without encountering
placeholder language or unverified claims.
